Description

In this project, we have implemented a simplified version of the paper Motion Consistency Model on datasets of cooking videos and images from the WebVid-2M (mini) and LAION-aesthetics. The training has been done in two different setups: Original setup of the paper (Setup-1), and a modified setup for preventing instability in training (Setup-2). Here, we would like to provide side-by-side visualizations of the generated videos, but more details on the training setups can be found here.

Metrics and Scores

To evaluate the performance of our student models, we used the commonly known metric Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) on a sub sample of less than 100 generated-real video pairs on same captions. We utilized an open repository for this calculation, but surprisingly we observed FVD increase (getting worse) by increasing the number of inference steps while the generated videos were improving in motion consistency and image quality.

The FVD calculation was done on subsets of cooking-related videos from both the WebVid and MSR-VTT datasets but the issue remained unchanged. This conflict with our expectations can be attributed to the quite small size of our evaluation video pairs in addition to the problems of FVD score reported by previous research (JEDi). The issue probably arises from the rejection of the Gaussianity assumptions about I3D features of videos required for FVD calculation exacerbated by inefficiency of FVD score in terms of sample size requirements to provide stable scores.

Due to this matter, we also calculated a new proposed metric (JEDi) that has been claimed to greatly address reported problems of FVD metric while improving the alignment with human perception. In contrast to the FVD scores, the JEDi scores improved (decreased in value) by increasing the number of inference steps, affirming our expectations. Table 5: JEDi Score Comparison

As shown in Table 5, JEDi scores has been able to capture video quality improvements by increasing inference steps in addition to per model comparisons that align with human perceptions. In both setups of training, generated videos in 4 steps have provided the best results. The reason of outperforming 4-step generations than 8-step generations can be attributed to excessive refinements and a decrease in dynamism of frames that together contribute to moving apart from real videos distribution.
